# Prompt Wording and Scientific Conclusion Stability

A controlled study of whether meaning-preserving prompt paraphrases affect scientific conclusions generated by instruction-tuned language models from the same scientific abstract.

## Research questions

1. Under deterministic decoding, how do three meaning-preserving paraphrases of the same source-only instruction affect source fidelity and substantive stability of one-sentence scientific conclusions generated by Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct and Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct from the same abstract?
2. How well does lexical similarity between generated conclusions align with source-grounded judgments of substantive stability?

## Experimental design

| Factor | Conditions |
|---|---|
| Scientific sources | 8 publisher-checked abstracts |
| Prompt wording | P0 reference, P1 paraphrase, P2 paraphrase |
| Models | Qwen2.5-0.5B-Instruct, Qwen2.5-1.5B-Instruct |
| Generation | Greedy decoding (`do_sample=false`), `max_new_tokens=100`, seed 42 recorded before each generation |
| Total | 8 × 3 × 2 = **48 outputs** |

The comparison changes the opening task wording while keeping the supplied abstract, common instructions, output format, model revision and decoding settings fixed within each condition. Both models belong to the same Qwen2.5 family; this is a within-family model-size comparison, not a comparison across independent model families. No model training or fine-tuning was conducted.

### Prompt conditions

- **P0 reference:** “State the central conclusion of the scientific abstract below in exactly one sentence.”
- **P1 paraphrase:** “In one sentence, report the main conclusion of the scientific abstract below.”
- **P2 paraphrase:** “Write a single-sentence account of the principal conclusion drawn in the scientific abstract below.”

## Main-run design and interpretation

The main experiment generated 48 outputs. The first-pass annotation report records mean output-quality scores of 0.618 for Qwen2.5-0.5B and 0.757 for Qwen2.5-1.5B. Across 48 prompt-pair comparisons, it records 25 stable, 15 emphasis shifts, eight substantive shifts and zero contradictions. These are descriptive results from AI-assisted annotations, not independent human ground truth. They require source-grounded human review before final submission.

## Important limitations

- Only eight purposively selected abstracts were used; the sample is not representative of scientific literature.
- Abstract-only inputs may omit context from full papers.
- The two models are from the same Qwen2.5 family.
- One greedy generation was collected per source × prompt × model condition; stochastic run-to-run variation was not measured.
- Three outputs did not follow the one-sentence format and were retained rather than silently edited.
- Quality ratings and pairwise labels are AI-assisted and still require human review.
- TF-IDF cosine measures lexical overlap, not scientific meaning or truth.
- The PSS-style score is a study-specific adaptation inspired by ProSA/PSS, not a full replication of the published benchmark.
